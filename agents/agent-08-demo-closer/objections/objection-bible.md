# The Call Taker — Objection Bible
## Word-for-Word Responses to Every Demo Objection

**Framework for Every Objection:**
1. **Acknowledge** — Show you heard them and validate their concern
2. **Reframe** — Shift their perspective on the issue
3. **Respond** — Give the specific answer
4. **Redirect** — Move them toward the close

---

## OBJECTION 01: "It's too expensive" / "I can't afford that"

**ACKNOWLEDGE:**
"I totally get it — $497 a month is real money, and you have to make sure every dollar you spend is worth it."

**REFRAME:**
"But here's how I think about it: this isn't an expense, it's an investment that pays for itself. Let me show you the math."

**RESPOND:**
"If you're getting even 20 calls a month that go to voicemail, and just 1 of those would've been a job... what's that job worth? $300? $500? $1,000? The AI only needs to help you book ONE extra job a month and it's paid for itself. Everything after that is profit."

"And compare that to the alternatives: a receptionist costs $2,500+ a month. An answering service charges $3-5 per call, which adds up to $500-800 a month easy. This is $497, flat rate, unlimited calls."

**REDIRECT:**
"So the question isn't really 'Can I afford this?' — it's 'Can I afford to keep missing calls?' Does that make sense?"

## OBJECTION 02: "I already have a receptionist"

**ACKNOWLEDGE:**
"That's great — having someone answer your phones is huge. A lot of companies don't even have that."

**REFRAME:**
"But let me ask: what happens after hours? Weekends? Holidays? When your receptionist is sick or on vacation?"

**RESPOND:**
"That's where this helps. The AI doesn't replace your receptionist — it backs them up. It handles after-hours calls, overflow when your receptionist is busy, and weekends. Think of it as a 24/7 safety net so you never miss a call, even when your receptionist isn't there."

"And honestly, it also takes some pressure off your receptionist. They can focus on walk-ins, paperwork, and more complex stuff, while the AI handles the straightforward 'I need an appointment' calls."

**REDIRECT:**
"Does your receptionist work nights and weekends? If not, this fills that gap. Make sense?"

## OBJECTION 03: "I don't trust AI"

**ACKNOWLEDGE:**
"I get it — AI can feel gimmicky, and you've probably seen some bad examples of it."

**REFRAME:**
"But here's the thing: this isn't some general AI chatbot. We built this specifically for HVAC companies. It's trained to ask the right questions, sound professional, and handle calls the way a real receptionist would."

**RESPOND:**
"That's why I want to call it together right now, so you can hear it yourself. I'm not asking you to trust me — I'm asking you to trust your own ears. Let's call the demo line and you tell me if it sounds like AI or like a real person."

*Make the demo call.*

"See? That's what your customers will hear. Did that sound robotic to you, or did it sound like someone who knew what they were doing?"

**REDIRECT:**
"I'm not asking you to trust AI in general. I'm asking: did that specific call sound good enough to answer your phones? What do you think?"

**RESPOND:**

**REDIRECT:**
"The question isn't 'Is it AI?' — it's 'Does it give my customers a good experience?' And based on what you just heard, does it?"

## OBJECTION 05: "Let me think about it"

**ACKNOWLEDGE:**
"Totally fair — this is a business decision, and you should take the time you need."

**REFRAME:**
"But let me ask: what's the main thing you're unsure about? Is it the price, the technology, the setup, or something else?"

*Listen. Address the specific concern. Then:*

**RESPOND:**
"I appreciate you being honest. Here's what I'd suggest: let me send you the proposal so you have everything in writing — pricing, ROI breakdown, how setup works, all of it. Take a couple days to think it over."

"But here's the thing: every day you wait is another day of missed calls. I'm not trying to pressure you — I just want you to think about how many calls went to voicemail this week, and how many of those could've been jobs."

**REDIRECT:**
"How about this: I'll send you the proposal today. You take a look, and I'll follow up with you on [SPECIFIC DAY]. Does morning or afternoon work better for a quick call?"

*Lock in the follow-up time.*

**REFRAME:**

**RESPOND:**
"But here's what I can do: we don't lock you into a contract. It's month-to-month, and you can cancel anytime. So if you sign up and it doesn't work for you in the first 30 days, you can cancel and you're only out $497. That's basically a trial — you're just paying for the first month upfront."

"And honestly, I'm confident enough that this will work for you that I'm not worried about you canceling. I think once you see it in action for a month, you're going to wonder how you ever ran your business without it."

**REDIRECT:**
"So the real question is: are you willing to invest $497 to test this for 30 days and see if it books you even one extra job? Because if it does, it's already paid for itself."

**ACKNOWLEDGE:**
"I hear you — if you're only getting a handful of calls a week, it might feel like overkill."

**REFRAME:**
"But here's the thing: small businesses actually benefit the most from this, because every missed call hurts more. If you're a big company with 50 calls a day, missing one isn't a big deal. But if you're getting 10 calls a week and you miss 3 of them, that's 30% of your potential jobs gone."

**RESPOND:**
"And the cost is the same whether you're getting 10 calls a week or 100. $497 a month is flat-rate, unlimited calls. So if you're small, you're actually getting a better deal than the big guys."

"Think about it this way: you're trying to grow, right? This helps you punch above your weight. A customer calls you and a bigger competitor at the same time. You answer immediately. They go to voicemail. Who do you think gets the job?"

**REDIRECT:**
"So being small isn't a reason not to do this — it's actually the best reason to do it. You need every call you can get, right?"

"When do you think you'll have a chance to talk to them? I'll follow up after that."

**Examples:** "Can it schedule estimates in my ServiceTitan calendar?" "Can it text customers confirmations?" "Can it speak Spanish?"

**ACKNOWLEDGE:**
"That's a great question. Right now, we don't have that specific feature built out."

**REFRAME:**
"But here's what we can do: we integrate with GoHighLevel, which can sync with most major CRM and scheduling tools. So even if the AI doesn't directly book into ServiceTitan, it can capture the info and feed it into your system."

**RESPOND (if you're building it):**
"And honestly, you're not the first person to ask about [FEATURE]. We're actually working on adding that in the next few months. So if you sign up now, you'll get it as soon as it's ready — no extra cost."

**RESPOND (if you're NOT building it):**
"I hear you — that would be really valuable. Let me ask: if we didn't have [FEATURE], but everything else worked great, would that be a dealbreaker? Or is the core value — answering every call, collecting info, booking appointments — still worth it?"

*Listen. If it's a dealbreaker, acknowledge it:*

"Totally fair. If that's a must-have for you, we might not be the right fit yet. But if that changes or if we add it down the road, I'll reach out. Cool?"

**REDIRECT (if it's not a dealbreaker):**
"So it sounds like [FEATURE] would be nice to have, but it's not make-or-break. The main thing is making sure you're answering every call and not losing jobs to voicemail, right?"

## BONUS: The "Silent Treatment" Objection

**YOU:**
"I know I just threw a lot at you. What are you thinking?"

*Wait. Let them talk. Don't fill the silence.*

---

## KEY PRINCIPLES

1. **Never argue.** Acknowledge their concern first.
2. **Ask questions.** Objections are usually surface-level. Dig deeper to find the real issue.
3. **Use their words.** If they say "I'm worried about cost," say "I get it — cost is important" not "I understand your concern."
4. **Redirect to the close.** Every objection response should end with a question that moves them forward.
5. **The demo call is your best weapon.** When in doubt, let them hear the AI.

**You've got this. Objections aren't rejections — they're just questions that need better answers.**
