# The Call Taker — ROI Talk Track
## How to Walk Through the Math That Makes Them Say Yes

**Purpose:** Turn abstract value into concrete dollars. Show them that $497/month isn't a cost — it's an investment that pays for itself with ONE extra job.

**When to Use This:** During the demo (minutes 10-13), after they've heard the AI and before you ask for the sale.

---

## THE SETUP

**YOU:** "Okay, so you've heard how it works. Now let me show you why this is a no-brainer from a numbers perspective. I want to walk through the ROI specific to your business, because I want you to see exactly how this pays for itself. Cool?"

*Wait for agreement. Pull up your notes or a calculator.*

---

## STEP 1: HOW MANY CALLS DO YOU GET?

**YOU:** "So first question: how many calls would you say you get in a typical week? Just ballpark it for me."

**What They Might Say:**
- "I don't know, maybe 20-30?"
- "Probably 50-60, we're pretty busy."
- "Not that many — maybe 10-15."

**YOUR RESPONSE (adapt to their answer):**
- If they're unsure: "No worries, let's just estimate. You're running a business, so you're probably getting at least 20-30 calls a week, right? Let's say 25 for easy math."
- If they give a range: Pick the lower end to be conservative. "Okay, so let's say 30 calls a week to be conservative."
- If they say a specific number: Use it. "Got it, so 40 calls a week."

**Write it down or say it out loud:** "Okay, so about [X] calls per week."

---

## STEP 2: HOW MANY GO TO VOICEMAIL?

**YOU:** "And of those [X] calls a week, how many do you think go to voicemail? Either because you're on a job site, it's after hours, you're driving, whatever. What percentage would you say?"

**What They Might Say:**
- "Honestly, probably half."
- "Not that many — maybe 20-30%?"
- "I don't know, I try to answer everything."

**YOUR RESPONSE (adapt to their answer):**

**If they give a percentage:**
"Okay, so about [Y%]. That's pretty common. So out of [X] calls a week, that's about [X × Y%] calls going to voicemail every week."

**If they say "not that many":**
"I hear you — you're pretty good about answering. But let's be honest: you can't be available 24/7. If you get calls after 5pm, or on weekends, or while you're on a ladder... those probably go to voicemail, right? Let's say 30% just to be conservative. So that's [X × 30%] calls a week."

**If they say "I answer everything":**
"That's impressive — but what about after hours? Like, if someone calls at 8pm on a Saturday because their AC died, are you answering that?"
*They'll usually admit those go to voicemail.*
"Right. So let's just say 20-30% of your calls go to voicemail because of timing. That's still [X × 20-30%] calls a week."

**Do the math out loud:**
"So [X] calls a week, [Y%] go to voicemail... that's about [Z] calls a week, or about [Z × 4] calls a month going to voicemail. Let's round to [nearest 5 or 10]."

**Example:**
- 40 calls/week × 40% = 16 calls/week → 64 calls/month → round to 60 calls/month

---

## STEP 3: HOW MANY OF THOSE WOULD'VE BEEN JOBS?

**YOU:** "Okay, so about [Z] calls a month going to voicemail. Now, not every call is a job — some are existing customers asking a quick question, some are people just price shopping, some are wrong numbers. But let's be conservative and say 1 out of every 10 of those missed calls was a real job you could've booked. That's [Z ÷ 10] jobs per month."

**Why 1 in 10 is conservative:**
"And honestly, that's probably low. If someone's calling an HVAC company, they usually need something. But let's stick with 1 in 10 to be safe."

**Write it down or say it out loud:** "So [Z ÷ 10] missed jobs per month. Make sense so far?"

---

## STEP 4: WHAT'S YOUR AVERAGE JOB WORTH?

**YOU:** "Now, what's your average job worth? Like, if someone calls for an AC repair, a maintenance call, or a furnace issue, what do you typically charge?"

**What They Might Say:**
- "It depends — could be $200, could be $5,000."
- "Probably $400-500 for most service calls."
- "I don't know, it varies a lot."

**YOUR RESPONSE (adapt to their answer):**

**If they give a range:**
"Got it. So let's just use the low end to be conservative. Let's say $400 per job on average."

**If they say it varies:**
"Totally fair — it depends on what the job is. But let's just pick a number for the math. What would you say is a typical service call? $300? $500?"
*Let them pick. Use their number.*

**If they hesitate:**
"No worries, I'll just use industry averages. Most HVAC service calls are somewhere between $300-800. Let's say $500 to be middle-of-the-road."

**Use these benchmarks if they're unsure:**
- **AC repair:** $300-800
- **Furnace repair:** $300-800
- **Maintenance call:** $150-300
- **Emergency call:** $500-1,200
- **System installation:** $5,000-15,000

**For the math, use a CONSERVATIVE number** (e.g., $400-500) so they can't say you're inflating it.

---

## STEP 5: THE PUNCH LINE — SHOW THEM THE LOST REVENUE

**YOU:** "Okay, so let's do the math. You're missing about [Z ÷ 10] jobs a month because those calls went to voicemail. And each job is worth about [$XXX]. So that's [Z ÷ 10 × $XXX] in potential revenue you're losing every single month."

**Pause. Let that number sink in.**

**YOU:** "That's [$XXX × 12] per year."

**Pause again.**

**YOU:** "And that's being conservative. We said 1 in 10 missed calls becomes a job, and we used your low-end pricing. The real number is probably higher."

**Example:**
- 60 missed calls/month ÷ 10 = 6 jobs
- 6 jobs × $500 = $3,000/month
- $3,000 × 12 = $36,000/year

---

## STEP 6: THE COMPARISON — $497 VS. THE ALTERNATIVES

**YOU:** "Now, The Call Taker costs $497 a month. So if this helps you book just ONE extra job per month — just one — it's already paid for itself. Everything after that is pure profit."

**Then compare to alternatives:**

**YOU:** "And compare that to your other options:
- Hiring a receptionist? That's $15 an hour, 40 hours a week... $2,500 a month minimum. And they're only there during business hours.
- Traditional answering service? Most charge $2-5 per call. If you're getting 100-200 calls a month, that's $400-1,000 a month, and they're usually generic — they're answering calls for dentists, lawyers, everyone.
- Doing nothing and just letting calls go to voicemail? You just saw the math — that's costing you [$XXX] per month."

**YOU:** "So $497 for 24/7 coverage, unlimited calls, built specifically for HVAC? That's the best deal out there."

---

## STEP 7: THE TIME SAVINGS (BONUS)

**If they're still on the fence, add this:**

**YOU:** "And that's just the revenue side. Let's talk about your time. How much is your time worth? Let's say $50 an hour, which is probably low for a business owner."

**YOU:** "Right now, you're stopping what you're doing to answer calls, or you're spending your evenings returning voicemails. How much time per week would you say you spend on the phone with customers just trying to schedule stuff? 5 hours? 10 hours?"

*Let them estimate.*

**YOU:** "Let's say 5 hours a week. That's 20 hours a month. At $50 an hour, that's $1,000 worth of your time every month just answering phones."

**YOU:** "What if you could get that time back and focus on running jobs, managing your team, or just going home at a decent hour? What's that worth to you?"

*Pause. Let them think.*

---

## STEP 8: THE CLOSE

**YOU:** "So here's the bottom line: you're losing [$XXX] a month in missed jobs, plus [X] hours of your time. The Call Taker costs $497 and solves both problems. It answers every call, books the appointments, and gives you your time back."

**YOU:** "Does that make sense? Can you see how this pays for itself?"

*Wait for response.*

**If they say yes:**
"Awesome. Let me walk you through what happens next."

**If they hesitate:**
"What's holding you back? Is it the price, the technology, the setup, or something else?"
*Address the objection. Reference the Objection Bible.*

## SCRIPTS FOR DIFFERENT COMPANY SIZES

### Small Company (5-15 calls/week, owner-operated)
**YOU:** "You're probably getting about 10-15 calls a week, right? And maybe 30-40% go to voicemail because you're on job sites or it's after hours. That's about 20-25 missed calls a month. If even 2 of those would've been jobs at $400 each, that's $800. The Call Taker costs $497. So it pays for itself and then some."

### Medium Company (30-60 calls/week, small team)
**YOU:** "You're probably getting 40-50 calls a week. Let's say 40% go to voicemail — that's about 80 missed calls a month. If 1 in 10 of those was a real job, that's 8 jobs. At $500 per job, that's $4,000 in lost revenue every month. The Call Taker costs $497. You see the math?"

### Larger Company (100+ calls/week)
**YOU:** "At your scale, you're probably getting 100+ calls a week. Even if only 20% go to voicemail, that's 80+ missed calls a month. If just 10% of those would've converted, that's 8+ jobs at $500-1,000 each. That's $4,000-8,000 in lost revenue. For $497, this is a no-brainer."

---

## KEY REMINDERS

1. **Use THEIR numbers whenever possible.** The more personalized the math, the more real it feels.
2. **Be conservative.** Use low-end estimates so they can't argue you're inflating the value.
3. **Pause after the big number.** Let the lost revenue sink in before you move to the close.
4. **Compare to alternatives.** $497 sounds expensive until you compare it to $2,500/month for a receptionist.
5. **Make it visual.** If you're screen-sharing, type the math into a notepad or calculator so they see it happening live.

**This is the moment they decide. Nail the ROI, and the sale closes itself.**
