Company Overview

The Call Taker — At a Glance

The Call Taker is an AI receptionist platform for service businesses. It answers every incoming phone call 24 hours a day, books appointments, handles emergencies, and sends the business owner a text summary — starting at $97 a month.

The platform serves 17 industries including HVAC, plumbing, electrical, dental, legal, roofing, locksmith, property management, veterinary, and more. It is built on GoHighLevel and powered by GPT-4o, with four custom-built Python automation engines running continuously in the background.

The Call Taker was founded in Nashville, Tennessee by Wallace Dobbs, who built the entire platform at 16 years old using Claude Code, Anthropic's AI coding tool. No venture capital. No employees. No co-working space.

Wallace Dobbs

Founder & CEO, The Call Taker — Age 16, Nashville TN

Wallace Dobbs is a 16-year-old self-taught programmer and entrepreneur from Nashville, Tennessee. He is the founder and sole builder of The Call Taker, an AI receptionist platform for service businesses.

He built the entire platform — the voice AI, four custom Python engines, 60+ automation scripts, 82 website pages, and a multi-channel outreach system — using Claude Code, Anthropic's AI coding tool. He handles all product development, sales, and operations himself.

Wallace is believed to be one of the youngest SaaS founders in the AI receptionist space. He runs the business on a bootstrapped budget with no outside funding, no employees, and no business degree.

His founding thesis: small businesses lose thousands of dollars every month to missed calls. The existing solutions — human answering services like Smith.ai and Ruby — charge $2,800-$5,100 per month, pricing out the businesses that need it most. The Call Taker starts at $97.

What makes this worth covering

1

A teenager built a real SaaS company — not a class project

Wallace isn't in a startup accelerator or submitting to a pitch competition. He built a production-grade platform with real automation infrastructure, a live demo line, and a real pricing model. It runs 24/7 on one Mac in Nashville.

2

He used AI to build it — and that's the whole story

The Call Taker was built almost entirely with Claude Code, Anthropic's AI coding tool. A 16-year-old with no CS degree used AI to out-build companies with 50-person engineering teams. That's the future of software development playing out in real time.

3

$97 vs. $5,100 — disrupting an industry that hasn't changed in decades

Human answering services have charged small businesses $2,800-$5,100 a month for 20 years. The Call Taker delivers the same service for $97 using AI. The pricing disruption alone is a story.

4

The missed call economy — billions lost every year by small businesses

An HVAC company in a metro area can lose $8,000-$14,000 a month in missed jobs. A solo plumber loses $500 in emergency calls every week they don't answer. This is a real economic problem affecting millions of small businesses in America.

5

What happens when a 16-year-old doesn't wait for permission

Most teenagers are told to wait — go to college, get experience, then start a business. Wallace ignored that. The result is a live product, a live demo line, and a business model built to scale to $1M+ ARR.
