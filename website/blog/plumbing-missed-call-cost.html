How Much Do Plumbers Lose from Missed Emergency Calls? ($5,400/Month)

February 24, 2026 · 7 min read · By The Call Taker Team

It is 11:30 PM. A pipe just burst in a homeowner's basement. Water is everywhere. They grab their phone and start calling plumbers. The first number goes to voicemail. They do not leave a message -- they call the next one. And the next one. The first plumber who picks up gets a $600+ emergency job. The plumbers who did not answer? They will never know the call happened.

This scenario plays out thousands of times every night across the country, and the cost of missed emergency calls for plumbers is far higher than most business owners calculate. Here is the real data.

47% of Plumbing Calls Come After Hours

Plumbing is one of the most emergency-driven trades in the service industry. Data from field service management platforms like ServiceTitan and Housecall Pro shows that 47% of all inbound plumbing calls happen outside normal business hours -- evenings, weekends, and holidays.

Unlike many other trades, plumbing emergencies cannot wait. A burst pipe causes $5,000-$70,000 in water damage per hour if left unchecked. A sewage backup creates a health hazard. A failed water heater leaves a family without hot water. These callers are desperate, they are ready to pay premium rates, and they will hire the first plumber who answers.

The Average Plumbing Call Is Worth $450

According to HomeAdvisor and plumbing industry benchmarks, the average plumbing service call generates $450 in revenue. Emergency calls command even higher rates -- typically $550-$800 when you factor in after-hours premiums, urgent parts markup, and the complexity of emergency repairs.

Let us use the conservative $450 figure and build out the monthly loss:

  • Average missed calls per week for a 2-5 tech plumbing company: 8-14
  • 85% will not call back (industry standard from BrightLocal data)
  • Monthly lost customers: 7-12 per week x 4 weeks x 85% = 24-41 lost customers
  • Monthly revenue loss at $450 avg: $10,800-$18,450

Even at the conservative end with just 3 missed calls per week:

The Types of Calls You Are Missing

Not all missed plumbing calls are created equal. Here are the most common emergency calls that go to voicemail after hours, ranked by urgency and value:

  • Burst pipes: Average job value $650-$1,200. Extremely time-sensitive -- every minute of delay means more water damage. These callers will pay any price for immediate response
  • Sewage backups: Average job value $500-$900. Health hazard that drives immediate action. Homeowners are panicked and will not wait for a callback
  • Water heater failures: Average job value $400-$2,500 (repair vs. replacement). Often discovered in the morning when there is no hot water. Callers want same-day service
  • Overflowing toilets: Average job value $250-$500. High urgency, especially with only one bathroom. These callers try 2-3 plumbers before giving up
  • Gas line leaks: Average job value $800-$1,500. Safety emergency. Callers often call the gas company and a plumber simultaneously
  • Slab leaks: Average job value $2,000-$4,000. Less urgent but extremely high value. Usually discovered during business hours but follow-up calls happen evenings and weekends

When Plumbing Emergencies Happen (Timing Data)

Understanding when calls come in reveals why so many are missed. Based on industry call-tracking data:

  • 6 AM - 8 AM (18% of emergency calls): Homeowners discover problems in the morning -- no hot water, backed-up drains, leaking pipes. These calls hit before most offices open
  • 5 PM - 10 PM (24% of emergency calls): Families come home and discover issues, or problems emerge during evening use. Offices are closed
  • 10 PM - 6 AM (12% of emergency calls): True middle-of-the-night emergencies. Burst pipes in winter, sewage backups, flooding. Highest urgency, highest willingness to pay
  • Weekends (28% of weekly calls): Heavy home use, more time to notice problems, and most plumbing offices are closed. A huge revenue gap for companies without coverage

Add it up and over 47% of your most valuable calls are coming when nobody is there to answer.

The Competition Factor: Your Competitors Are Answering

The plumbing industry is fiercely competitive, especially in metropolitan areas. A homeowner searching "emergency plumber near me" will see 5-10 options. They do not call one and wait. They call multiple plumbers simultaneously until someone picks up.

The 3-call rule: Research shows that homeowners with plumbing emergencies typically call up to 3 plumbers before hiring one. The company that answers first wins the job approximately 80% of the time. By the third call, they have hired someone and the other two never get a chance.

This means your marketing spend -- Google Ads, SEO, truck wraps, yard signs -- is generating leads that you are paying to acquire and then losing to voicemail. Every missed call has a cost of acquisition behind it that you already paid.

Calculating Your Own Annual Loss

Here is a simple way to estimate your plumbing company's missed-call revenue loss:

  1. Check your phone system for the number of missed calls in the last 30 days
  2. Multiply by 0.85 -- the percentage who will not call back
  3. Multiply by $450 (average ticket) or by your own average ticket
  4. Multiply by 12 for your annual loss

Example: 20 missed calls/month x 0.85 = 17 lost customers x $450 = $7,650/month or $91,800/year. For a company with higher call volume or higher average tickets, the number easily exceeds six figures.
