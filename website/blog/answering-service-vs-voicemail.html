Answering Service vs Voicemail: The Real Cost Difference

By Wallace Dobbs · 2026-03-29 · The Call Taker

Every business owner has had this debate: "Do I really need an answering service, or is voicemail good enough?"

The short answer: voicemail is free, but it's not good enough. Not even close. Here's why the numbers don't lie.

The Voicemail Problem

85% of callers who hit voicemail will never call back. They don't leave a message. They don't try again tomorrow. They call the next business on Google and give them the job instead.

For a business that misses 6 calls per week (the average), that's 24 lost customers per month. At $200 per job, that's $4,800 per month in lost revenue. At $400 per job, it's $9,600.

Voicemail isn't free. It just hides the cost.

What an Answering Service Actually Does

An answering service picks up the phone when you can't. Some use human operators. Some use automated systems. The good ones book appointments, take messages, and route emergencies.

The difference between voicemail and an answering service is the difference between losing a customer and keeping one. That single distinction is worth thousands of dollars per month to any business that depends on phone calls.

Traditional Answering Services

Human-staffed call centers charge $1 to $3 per minute. They answer after 15 to 30 seconds. They read from scripts. They're professional but limited: they take a message, they don't book appointments, they don't know your business inside out.

Monthly cost: $300 to $1,200 depending on call volume. Unpredictable billing.
