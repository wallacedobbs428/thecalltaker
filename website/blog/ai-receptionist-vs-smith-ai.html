Smith.ai Pricing: What You Actually Pay

Smith.ai's published pricing looks manageable until you understand the per-call billing model. Here is what their plans look like as of early 2026:

  • Starter: ~$285/month for 30 calls. That is $9.50 per call.
  • Basic: ~$465/month for 50 calls. Calls over 50: ~$9.30 each.
  • Pro: ~$690/month for 100 calls. Calls over 100: ~$6.90 each.
  • Enterprise: $1,000+/month, negotiated pricing, higher call volumes.

The math seems straightforward on a slow month. The problem is that service businesses do not have slow months that are predictable in advance. An HVAC company in July during a heat wave might get three times their normal call volume. A law firm that runs a single ad gets flooded with intake calls. A plumber after a winter storm gets 80 calls in two days.

Every one of those calls above your plan limit costs $7–$10. That is not a small fee — that is significant money on top of a bill you already budgeted for.

$285–$900+ Typical Smith.ai monthly range for service businesses — before overages during busy season

The Overage Problem (The $5,100 Horror Story)

We mentioned Ruby Receptionists in our virtual receptionist comparison piece — a business owner who received a $5,100 bill for a single month. This is not unique to Ruby. It happens across every per-call virtual receptionist service, including Smith.ai.

The pattern is always the same:

  1. Business signs up for the $285 Starter plan. Budgets $285/month.
  2. Business runs a Google Ads campaign, or has a good month, or experiences a seasonal spike.
  3. Call volume goes from 30 to 90 in a month.
  4. 60 overage calls at $9.50 each = $570 in overages.
  5. Total bill: $285 + $570 = $855 — 3x what was budgeted.
  6. Business owner gets the invoice. Shock. Then anger. Then cancellation.

The dirty secret of per-call billing: Virtual receptionist companies make the most money during your best business months. When you are busy and making money, your bill spikes. When you are slow, you pay for included minutes you did not use. You are paying a tax on your own success.

This is structurally backwards. Your phone answering cost should be a fixed, predictable business expense — not a variable that scales with your success.

Who Should Use What

Smith.ai makes more sense if:

  • You need a live human voice that callers can explicitly recognize as human
  • Your call volume is very low and extremely predictable (under 25 calls/month)
  • You handle highly sensitive legal, medical, or emotional intake that requires a human judgment layer
  • You have strong preferences for specific CRM integrations Smith.ai supports that The Call Taker does not

The Call Taker makes more sense if:

  • You run an HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing, dental, legal, or any other service business where call volume varies
  • You want to stop worrying about your phone bill during your busiest months
  • You need true 24/7 coverage without shift-change gaps or weekend staffing issues
  • You want the same quality call experience at 3 AM on a Saturday as at 10 AM on a Tuesday
  • You want to try before you commit

The bottom line for service businesses: The per-call model is fundamentally misaligned with your revenue cycle. Your busiest months — summer for HVAC, winter for plumbing, storm season for roofing — are exactly when your answering bill should stay predictable. It never does with per-call billing.
