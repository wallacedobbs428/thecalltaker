The True Cost of Missed Calls for Electrical Contractors (Data + Calculator)

February 24, 2026 · 8 min read · By Wallace Dobbs

A homeowner notices sparks coming from an outlet at 8:30 PM. They smell something burning. They grab their phone and search "emergency electrician near me." They call the first result -- voicemail. They call the second -- voicemail. The third company answers. That electrician just won a $400+ emergency service call, and the first two companies will never know the opportunity existed.

For electrical contractors, missed calls carry a unique weight because electrical problems are safety hazards. Callers are frightened, they are urgent, and they will not wait. This article lays out the true cost of missed calls for electrical contractors, with data and a calculator to estimate your own losses.

The Average Electrical Service Call Is Worth $400

According to HomeAdvisor and electrical industry benchmarks, the average electrical service call generates $400 in revenue. This includes the service fee, diagnostic charge, and repair work. The breakdown by job type shows significant variation:

  • Outlet/switch repair: $150-$350
  • Circuit breaker issues: $200-$500
  • Panel upgrades: $1,500-$4,000
  • Whole-home rewiring: $8,000-$15,000
  • Generator installation: $3,000-$7,000
  • Emergency service calls (after-hours): $350-$800
  • EV charger installation: $800-$2,500

Using the conservative $400 average, the losses from missed calls add up quickly.

Electrical Emergency Types That Drive After-Hours Calls

Electrical emergencies are particularly concerning because they can involve fire risk and safety hazards. This makes callers more desperate and less willing to wait. The most common after-hours electrical calls include:

  • Sparking outlets or switches: Visible sparks create immediate panic. Homeowners worry about fire and want someone there now. These calls command emergency premiums
  • Complete power outages (not utility-related): When the power is out but the neighbors' lights are on, homeowners need an electrician. These often indicate panel failures or main breaker issues -- big-ticket repairs
  • Burning smell from walls or panels: Perhaps the most frightening call type. The homeowner smells something burning but cannot find the source. Time-critical and high-value
  • Tripping breakers that won't reset: A breaker that trips repeatedly indicates a serious underlying issue. Homeowners call after trying to reset it multiple times
  • Exposed or damaged wiring: Storms, renovation work, or aging infrastructure expose live wires. Safety emergency requiring immediate response
  • Post-storm electrical issues: Lightning strikes, downed lines on property, and surge damage drive sudden call spikes
38% of electrical service calls come outside normal business hours

Seasonal Patterns in Electrical Call Volume

Electrical call volume follows distinct seasonal patterns that savvy contractors plan around:

  • Summer (June-August): AC systems overload circuits. Tripping breakers, flickering lights, and brownouts spike. Panel upgrade requests increase 40-60%. Average missed call value: $450
  • Winter (November-February): Space heater overloads, holiday lighting issues, and generator demand during ice storms. Call volume increases 25-35%. Average missed call value: $500 (includes generator calls)
  • Storm season (varies by region): Lightning strikes and power surges cause electrical damage. Call volume can spike 200-300% after major storms. Average missed call value: $600+
  • Spring/Fall: Lower emergency volume but higher project calls -- EV chargers, panel upgrades, outdoor electrical. Average missed call value: $350

The 85% Rule: Why Voicemail Is a Revenue Killer

The same industry data that applies across all service trades is particularly brutal for electricians: 85% of callers who reach voicemail will not call back or leave a message. For electrical emergencies specifically, the number may be even higher because:

  • Safety anxiety drives faster decision-making
  • Homeowners with sparking or burning smells do not have patience to wait for a callback
  • Google makes it easy to call the next electrician in seconds
  • Many electrical emergencies happen when the homeowner is already stressed (no power, visible sparks)
