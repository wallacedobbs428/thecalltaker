How Vet Clinics Lose Emergency Calls Every Night

Pet emergencies don't happen 9-5. Your biggest revenue opportunities come when nobody's answering your phone.

Dog Eats Chocolate at Midnight

Owner finds the ripped-open bag of baking chocolate and panics. They call your clinic -- voicemail. They call the next vet -- someone answers. That's an $800+ emergency visit with diagnostics, IV fluids, and monitoring gone to your competitor. They're not calling you back in the morning.

Cat Hit by Car on Sunday

A cat comes home limping with a broken leg on a Sunday morning. The owner is sobbing on the phone. This is a $1,200+ trauma case with X-rays, surgery, and follow-ups. They need someone NOW -- not a callback on Monday morning.

Puppy Seizures Saturday Night

A first-time puppy owner watches their 4-month-old start seizing on a Saturday night. They're terrified and calling every vet in town. The first clinic that answers gets a $900+ emergency workup, and probably a lifetime client. If you don't pick up, they'll never call you again.

Pet Owner Calls During Surgery

Your front desk is overwhelmed -- two phones ringing, three clients checking out, and a worried pet owner calling about their dog's vomiting. The call goes to voicemail. That owner Googles the next clinic and books there instead. Five missed calls a week during busy hours = $4,000+ in lost revenue every month.

How It Works for Veterinary Clinics

1

Pet Owner Calls About an Emergency

It's midnight and their dog just ate something toxic. Instead of voicemail, our AI receptionist answers with your clinic name and calmly gathers details -- what happened, what the pet ingested, the pet's breed and weight, and the owner's contact info.

2

We Triage and Alert Your Team

For emergencies, the receptionist contacts your on-call vet with the case details and sends an urgent text alert. For non-urgent calls like medication refills or appointment requests, it books the next available slot on your calendar.

3

You Get Full Case Details by Text

Within seconds you have the pet owner's name, phone number, pet details, symptoms, and urgency level. Your team knows exactly what's coming in. The pet owner feels heard and your clinic captures the revenue.
