The Problem

James Rivera is a solo plumber in Tampa. He's under a house fixing a slab leak, his phone rings, and he can't answer. By the time he calls back an hour later, the customer already found someone else.

"I'm a one-man show. When I'm on a job, I can't answer the phone. When I'm driving, I can't answer the phone. When I'm sleeping, I definitely can't answer the phone. But pipes don't care about business hours — they burst at 2 AM."

James estimated he was missing 8 to 10 calls per week. For a plumber doing emergency work at $250-$500 per job, that's $2,000 to $5,000 a week walking away.

Why The Call Taker

James tried everything. He set up voicemail with a "I'll call you back within 30 minutes" message. Call-back rate: about 40% — most people with a flooded kitchen aren't waiting 30 minutes. He tried an answering service but they couldn't explain his pricing or book emergency calls properly.

"The first time I heard the AI handle a call, I almost fell off my ladder. A lady called saying water was coming through her ceiling. The AI asked the right questions — is the water still running, have you turned off the main, what's your address — and booked her for an emergency visit. That's a $450 job I would have lost."

— James Rivera, Owner, Reliable Rooter
