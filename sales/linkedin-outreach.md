## 2. Connection Request Messages

LinkedIn connection requests have a 300-character limit. Every word counts. These are written to feel human, not automated. Do NOT pitch in the connection request. The only goal is to get them to accept.

---

### Message 1: The Local Neighbor Angle

> Hey [First Name] -- saw you run [Company Name] out of [City]. I am based in Brentwood and work with HVAC companies across Middle TN. Always great connecting with local business owners. Hope you are staying busy this season.

**Characters:** ~225

**When to use:** Default opener. Works for any HVAC owner in your target cities. The city reference makes it feel personal and local.

---

### Message 2: The Mutual Industry Angle

> Hi [First Name] -- I have been spending a lot of time in the HVAC space lately and your company caught my eye. Would love to connect and follow along with what you all are doing at [Company Name].

**Characters:** ~200

**When to use:** When you cannot find a personal detail or city reference. Works broadly. Low friction, easy to accept.

---

### Message 3: The Specific Compliment Angle

> [First Name] -- came across [Company Name] and noticed your Google reviews are really solid. You all are clearly doing right by your customers. Would love to connect -- I work in the HVAC space here in Tennessee.

**Characters:** ~215

**When to use:** When you have taken 30 seconds to look at their Google listing and can reference something specific. The compliment makes it feel personal and earns goodwill before you ever send a DM.

---

### Message 4: The Content Engagement Angle

> Hey [First Name] -- saw your post about [topic]. Good stuff. I work with HVAC owners in Middle Tennessee and it is always good to connect with people who share what they are learning. Looking forward to staying in touch.

**Characters:** ~225

**When to use:** When the person has recently posted or shared something on LinkedIn. Reference it specifically. This has the highest acceptance rate because they know you actually looked at their profile.

---

### Message 5: The Straight Shooter Angle

> [First Name] -- fellow Tennessee business owner here. I built a tool for HVAC companies and I am always looking to learn from owners who are in the trenches every day. No pitch, just like connecting with people in the industry.

**Characters:** ~230

**When to use:** When you want to be upfront that you work in the HVAC space without being salesy. The "no pitch" line disarms skepticism. Honest and direct.

---

**General Rules for Connection Requests:**

- Always use their first name
- Always reference their company name or city if possible
- Never mention The Call Taker by name in the request
- Never mention price, demos, or your website
- Never use phrases like "I'd love to pick your brain" or "let's synergize"
- Send 15-25 connection requests per day, max. LinkedIn will throttle or restrict your account if you go higher.
- Best days: Tuesday through Thursday. Best time: 7-9 AM Central.

## 3. Follow-Up DM Sequence

These messages go out AFTER they accept your connection request. Space them 2-3 days apart. The sequence builds trust before ever asking for anything.

---

### DM 1: The Thank You + Genuine Question
**Timing:** Same day they accept or the following morning

> Hey [First Name] -- appreciate you connecting. I am curious -- how long have you been running [Company Name]? I have been talking to a lot of HVAC owners around Middle TN lately and every single one has a different story about how they got started. Always interesting to hear.

**Purpose:** Opens a genuine conversation. Asking about their origin story makes them feel respected. Most business owners love talking about how they built what they have. You are building rapport, nothing more.

**If they respond:** Have a real conversation. Ask follow-up questions. Share something about your own journey. Do NOT pivot to a pitch. Let this be a human interaction. The relationship matters more than any single sale.

**If they do not respond:** No worries. Move to DM 2 on schedule. Some people do not check LinkedIn DMs regularly.

---

### DM 2: The Value Drop
**Timing:** 2-3 days after DM 1

> [First Name] -- random thought for you. I have been looking at data on missed calls in the HVAC industry and the numbers are kind of wild. The average HVAC company misses somewhere around 30-40% of incoming calls, especially after hours and on weekends. For a company doing decent volume, that can easily be $10K-$20K a month in lost revenue just walking out the door.
>
> I am not sure if that is something you have ever looked into for [Company Name], but I put together a free audit tool that shows HVAC owners exactly where their calls are falling through the cracks. No cost, no obligation, just data.
>
> If you are ever curious: thecalltaker.com/audit
>
> Either way, hope business is strong heading into [current season].

**Purpose:** This is a value-first message. You are leading with an industry insight (missed calls), not a product pitch. The audit offer is positioned as a free tool, not a sales funnel. The tone stays casual and zero-pressure.

**If they respond with interest:** Walk them through the audit. Be helpful. Answer questions. Let the data do the selling.

**If they respond but are not interested:** Thank them genuinely. Stay in their orbit. Like their posts. Be a presence, not a pest.

**If they do not respond:** Move to DM 3 on schedule.

---

### DM 3: The Soft Close + Graceful Exit
**Timing:** 2-3 days after DM 2

> Hey [First Name] -- last thing from me, I promise I am not going to be that guy who blows up your inbox.
>
> I built something called The Call Taker -- it is an AI receptionist designed specifically for HVAC companies. Answers every call 24/7, books appointments, captures leads. Runs $297 a month with no contract.
>
> I know that might not be something you need right now, and that is totally fine. But if you ever want to hear what it sounds like, you can call the demo line anytime: (615) 784-5747. It takes about 60 seconds and there is zero follow-up pressure.
>
> Regardless, it was good connecting with you. If you ever need anything HVAC-business related, I am always happy to share what I have been learning from other owners in the area.
>
> Talk soon -- Wallace

**Purpose:** This is your first and only direct mention of The Call Taker in the sequence. But notice the framing: you acknowledge it might not be the right time, you give them a low-friction way to engage (calling the demo line on their own terms), and you exit gracefully. The "I am not going to be that guy" line builds trust because that is exactly what every business owner fears when they accept a connection request from someone they do not know.

**After DM 3:** If they do not respond, stop sending DMs. Shift to the long game: engage with their content, like their posts, comment thoughtfully, stay visible. When they are ready, they will come to you. The seed has been planted.

### Content Post Best Practices

- **Post times:** Tuesday, Wednesday, and Thursday between 7-8 AM Central. HVAC owners scroll LinkedIn before the workday starts.

## 6. Comment Strategy

Commenting on other people's posts is the single most underrated LinkedIn growth strategy. A thoughtful comment on a high-visibility post puts your name, face, and headline in front of hundreds or thousands of people -- including HVAC owners who have never heard of you.

---

### Who to Follow and Engage With

**HVAC Industry Voices:**
- HVAC company owners who post regularly (search "HVAC owner" on LinkedIn and filter by "Posts")
- HVAC trade publication editors and writers (ACHR News, HVAC Insider, Contracting Business)
- HVAC supplier and distributor reps in Tennessee (Lennox, Carrier, Trane reps)
- People posting about ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, Jobber, and other HVAC software
- HVAC training organizations and trade schools in Middle Tennessee

**Small Business and Trades Voices:**
- Local Nashville and Middle Tennessee business leaders and Chamber of Commerce accounts
- Home services industry commentators and consultants
- Small business coaches who focus on trades and service businesses
- Other home services owners (plumbing, electrical, roofing) -- they face the same missed-call problem and their audiences overlap with yours

**AI and Technology Voices:**
- People posting about AI in small business and the trades
- Business automation thought leaders -- so you are positioned as part of a larger conversation, not just someone selling a product

---

### How to Comment Effectively

**Rule 1: Add value. Never just agree.**

Bad comment:
> "Great post! So true."

Good comment:
> "This is spot on. I have been talking to HVAC owners in Middle Tennessee and the missed-call problem is even worse than most people think. One owner told me he was losing 30+ calls a week during peak season and had no idea until he started tracking it. The awareness gap is real."

The good comment adds a specific data point, references your geography, and contributes to the conversation. It makes people curious about who you are.

---

**Rule 2: Tell a micro-story.**

When possible, share a brief anecdote from your experience. Stories are memorable. Facts are forgettable. Your comments should make people stop scrolling.

> "I called 50 HVAC companies in Tennessee as a test. Only 3 answered the phone in a way that made me feel like they wanted my business. The other 47 were voicemail, hold music, or someone who did not know the company's service area. The bar is low -- and that is actually a massive opportunity for owners who take phone experience seriously."

---

**Rule 3: Ask a follow-up question.**

Ending your comment with a question can spark a reply thread, which boosts visibility for both you and the original poster. People appreciate commenters who drive real discussion.

> "Really interesting take on this. Do you think most HVAC owners realize how many calls they are missing, or is it one of those blind spots that does not get attention until revenue dips?"

---

**Rule 4: Reference your geography.**

Casually drop Tennessee, Nashville, or your target cities when it fits naturally. This signals to local HVAC owners that you are in their world, not some random person on the internet.

> "Seeing this play out constantly with HVAC companies here in Middle Tennessee. The Nashville market especially is so competitive that one missed call can mean a $1,500 job going to the company down the street."

---

**Rule 5: Never pitch in the comments.**

Do not drop your website link or product name in other people's comment sections. It looks desperate and it will annoy the original poster. Let your headline and profile do the work. If someone is interested, they will click your name.

The one exception: if the original poster is specifically asking for tool recommendations or someone asks you directly what you do, then you can mention The Call Taker naturally.

---

### Daily Comment Routine

Spend 15-20 minutes per day on this. Consistency beats volume. Every single day.

**Morning routine (7:00-7:20 AM Central):**

1. Open LinkedIn. Check notifications first. Reply to any comments on your own posts -- this keeps your post engagement high.
2. Scroll your feed. Find 3-5 posts from people in the HVAC, home services, or local business space.
3. Leave a thoughtful comment on each one. Aim for 3-5 sentences minimum. No drive-by "great post" comments. Ever.
4. Like and reply to 2-3 comments that OTHER people left on those same posts. This gets you seen by their networks too.
5. Check if any HVAC owners in your target cities have posted anything new. If so, engage immediately. If they are not a connection yet, like the post, leave a comment, and send a connection request.

**Weekly targets:**
- 15-25 meaningful comments per week
- 5-10 new connection requests per week to HVAC owners in Middle Tennessee
- 2-3 original posts per week
- Reply to every single comment on your own posts within 4 hours

## The Golden Rule

Every interaction on LinkedIn should pass one simple test:

**"Would I say this to an HVAC owner if I ran into them at a gas station in Brentwood?"**

If the answer is no -- if it sounds too polished, too scripted, too much like a marketing email -- rewrite it until it sounds like Wallace talking to another business owner.

The HVAC owners you are trying to reach have been pitched a thousand times by marketing agencies, software companies, and consultants. They can smell a sales play from a mile away. They delete InMails without reading them. They ignore connection requests that feel automated. They scroll past posts that read like advertisements.

But they will stop for someone who is genuine. Someone who is local. Someone who actually understands what it is like to run an HVAC company in Middle Tennessee. Someone who leads with value and earns the right to eventually ask for their business.

Be that person. Be Wallace from Brentwood who built something useful and wants to help.

That is your competitive advantage. Nobody else in this space is from Tennessee. Nobody else is showing up at the same trade shows, eating at the same restaurants, dealing with the same weather. Nobody else is willing to sit across the table and have a real conversation without an agenda.

Lead with value. Build relationships. Let the product speak for itself.

The calls will come.
