## The Strategy

You call an HVAC company after hours. You screen-record what happens — the voicemail, the hold music, the "leave a message and we'll get back to you" robot. Then you flip to your demo line and show what The Call Taker does instead. You send the video directly to the owner via Facebook DM, Instagram DM, or LinkedIn message.

This works because you are not telling them they have a problem. You are showing them.

## 1. Pre-Recording Checklist

Before you sit down to record, get all of this ready so you can knock out 5-10 videos in one session.

### Research the Prospect

- [ ] Find the HVAC company's phone number (Google listing or website)
- [ ] Find the owner's name (Google, Facebook business page, LinkedIn, BBB listing)
- [ ] Check their Google reviews — note the star rating and review count
- [ ] Look for any negative reviews mentioning "couldn't reach anyone," "no one answered," "never called back" (gold if you find these)
- [ ] Find the owner on Facebook, Instagram, or LinkedIn so you know where you are sending the video
- [ ] Note the company name, city, and anything personal you can mention (truck wrap color, slogan, years in business, a recent job they posted about)

### Tech Setup

- [ ] Open Loom (or screen recording tool) — make sure webcam is on, screen share is ready
- [ ] Have your phone ready to make the call (use speakerphone so Loom picks up the audio)
- [ ] Have The Call Taker demo line number ready: **(615) 784-5747**
- [ ] Have thecalltaker.com open in a browser tab (you will show it briefly at the end)
- [ ] Close all other tabs and notifications — keep the screen clean
- [ ] Good lighting on your face — sit near a window or use a desk lamp
- [ ] Quiet room, no background noise

### Mindset

- You are a guy from the trades helping another guy from the trades. That is it.
- You are not pitching. You are showing something you built because you saw the same problem over and over.
- Talk like you are talking to a buddy at a supply house, not like you are reading a teleprompter.

## 5. Tips for Making It Feel Personal, Not Salesy

### DO:

- **Use their first name at least twice** — once at the start, once at the close. It is the difference between a mass blast and a message that was actually made for them.
- **Mention their city specifically.** "I see you guys are over in Mt. Juliet" or "right there off Lebanon Road" hits completely different than "I work with HVAC companies." These owners are proud of where they serve. Acknowledge it.
- **Reference something specific about their business.** Their truck wrap color, their slogan, a recent Google review, a Facebook post about a big install they just finished, the number of years they have been in business. This proves you actually looked them up and you are not blasting 200 people.
- **Record each video individually.** Do NOT batch record a generic video and swap out names. They can tell. One video, one prospect. Every time.
- **Be a little imperfect.** If you stumble on a word, leave it in. If your dog barks in the background, laugh it off and keep going. If you lose your train of thought for a second, say "anyway" and get back on track. Perfect videos feel like ads. Real videos feel like a real person who actually gives a damn.
- **Lean back in your chair.** Relax your shoulders. Talk like you are FaceTiming a friend who happens to own an HVAC company. Your body language communicates more than your words.
- **Let the voicemail play in full silence.** Do not talk over it. The awkward silence while their generic greeting plays is doing the selling for you. Let the dead air breathe.
- **Let the AI demo run long enough to be impressive.** Do not cut it short. The longer the AI sounds like a real, competent person, the harder the contrast hits.
- **End with "shoot me a message back" not "let's schedule a call."** Lower barrier. Less commitment. More responses.

### DO NOT:

- **Say "I'd love to hop on a quick call."** That sounds like every cold email they have ever deleted. Say "shoot me a message back" or "just reply here."
- **Use the words "solution," "leverage," "scale," "optimize," or "ROI."** You are not a SaaS bro. You are Wallace from Nashville. Talk like it.
- **Read from a script on camera.** Use the scripts above as a guide, internalize the flow, then talk naturally. Your eyes looking off-screen to read kills every ounce of trust you are building.
- **Send the same video to two owners who know each other.** Middle Tennessee is a small world. HVAC guys talk at supply houses, at trade shows, in Facebook groups. If two owners compare notes and realize they got the same video, you are done.
- **Trash-talk their current setup.** "Your voicemail is costing you money" is helpful. "Your voicemail is terrible" is insulting. Stay on the helping side of that line. Always.
- **Over-explain the AI technology.** Nobody cares about machine learning or natural language processing. They care that it picks up the phone, sounds good, and sends them the lead. Keep it simple.
- **Use fake urgency.** "Only 3 spots left in your area" is a lie and they know it. "I can only take on 5 more companies this month" — also a lie. Just be real. The product sells itself when you show it.
- **Start with "Hey, I hope this message finds you well."** That is the sound of an instant delete. Start with their name and get to the point.
- **Apologize for reaching out.** "Sorry to bother you" makes you sound like you do not believe in what you are selling. You have something that will make them more money. Own it.

### The Mindset Before You Hit Record:

You are a business owner in their area who found something that works and you are passing it along because you would want someone to do the same for you. Peer to peer. Trades guy to trades guy. You are not above them, not below them, not selling to them. You are showing them something and letting them decide. That energy comes through on camera more than any script ever could.

## 6. Best Times to Record and Send

### Best Times to RECORD the Videos

The whole strategy depends on their phone going to voicemail. You need to call when no one is there.

- **Weekday evenings between 6:00 PM and 8:30 PM** — Most HVAC offices close by 5:00 or 5:30. By 6:00 PM you are almost guaranteed to hit voicemail. This is your primary recording window.
- **Saturday mornings between 7:00 AM and 9:00 AM** — Many shops are closed or running a skeleton Saturday crew that may not answer the main line. This is a great time because homeowners absolutely call on Saturday mornings when they wake up to a cold house.
- **Sunday anytime** — Almost nobody is answering on a Sunday. And you can say in the video, "I called on a Sunday morning, which is exactly when a homeowner realizes their system went out overnight." That framing is powerful.
- **Lunch hour (12:00 - 1:00 PM weekdays)** — Riskier because someone might pick up, but a lot of one-person offices or small shops miss calls during lunch. If they answer, just politely ask a question about their services and move on to the next prospect.

**If someone actually answers:** Hang up politely or ask a quick question about their services and move on to a different prospect. Do not try to force the strategy if they are actually answering their phone — it only works when you catch the voicemail.

### Best Times to SEND the Videos

- **Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday between 6:30 AM and 8:00 AM** — HVAC owners check their phones early, usually before the first tech meeting or dispatch. You want to be sitting in their inbox when they pick up their phone with their coffee.
- **Tuesday through Thursday between 7:30 PM and 9:00 PM** — The day is done. They are on the couch scrolling Facebook or Instagram. This is when they actually have time to watch a video. This window works especially well for Facebook and Instagram DMs.
- **Sunday evening between 7:00 PM and 9:00 PM** — Some owners browse messages Sunday night to get a jump on Monday. A video sitting there waiting for them can work.
- **Avoid Mondays** — Everybody is catching up, putting out fires, dispatching the week. Your message gets buried under everything else.
- **Avoid Fridays** — People are mentally checked out by Friday afternoon. By Monday morning they have forgotten about your video entirely.
- **Avoid sending between 9:00 AM and 5:00 PM on weekdays** — They are in the middle of running their business. They see your message, think "I'll watch that later," and never do.

### The Batch Recording Strategy

Set aside one evening per week. Tuesday or Wednesday night, 6:30 to 8:30 PM works best.

**Before that session:**
- Research 8-10 companies earlier in the day (fill out the pre-recording checklist for each one)
- Line up their phone numbers, owner names, and the platform you will DM them on
- Have everything tabbed and ready so you are not researching between recordings

**During the session:**
- Record all 8-10 videos in one sitting
- The first couple will take 5-7 minutes each as you find your rhythm
- By video five or six you will be doing them in one or two takes
- Total session time: about 90 minutes for 8-10 personalized videos

**The next morning (Wednesday or Thursday, 6:30-7:30 AM):**
- Send all the DMs with the Loom links
- Log each one in your tracking sheet

**That gives you 8-10 personalized outreach videos per week. At a 10-15% response rate, that is 1-2 conversations per batch. At a 20-30% close rate on conversations, you are signing 1-2 new clients every 2-3 weeks from this channel alone.**

**Loom tells you:**
- Whether they watched the video
- How much of it they watched
- Exactly when they watched it

**Use that data:**
- If someone watches the full video but does not respond, your Day 2 follow-up has a strong chance of converting them. They are interested. They just have not committed yet. A gentle nudge is all they need.
- If someone watches only the first 10-15 seconds and drops off, your thumbnail or opening hook did not land. Look at what you said in those first 10 seconds and adjust.
- If someone watches it multiple times, they are very interested. Move your follow-up earlier — do not wait until Day 2. Send it that same evening or the next morning.
- If the video is never opened at all, the DM message itself did not get their attention. Try a different platform or rewrite your intro message.
