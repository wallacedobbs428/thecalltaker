## Part 2: Rules of Engagement (Do Not Get Banned)

### The Golden Rules

1. **Lurk for 7 days before posting.** Read the room. Understand the culture, the inside jokes, the hot topics. Every group has a personality.

2. **Your first 10 interactions should be comments, not posts.** Help people. Answer questions. Be the guy who knows things.

3. **Never drop a link in your first month.** If someone asks what you do, tell them to DM you. Links in comments trigger spam filters and admin suspicion.

4. **Optimize your personal Facebook profile.** When people see your helpful comments, they will click your name. Your profile should say who you are, what The Call Taker is, and link to the website. Let the profile do the selling.

5. **80/20 Rule:** 80% of your activity should be genuine engagement with zero mention of your product. 20% can be soft-touch content that relates to the problem you solve (missed calls, after-hours coverage).

6. **Never argue with admins.** If a post gets removed, don't complain. Adjust and try again differently.

7. **Build relationships with group admins.** They are often HVAC influencers. If an admin likes you, they may even endorse your product.

8. **DM warm leads, don't pitch in comments.** When someone says "I'm losing calls" in a thread, comment with empathy and advice. Then DM: "Hey man, saw your post about missed calls. I went through the same thing. Mind if I share what I did?"

### Post 1: The Poll (High Engagement, Data Collection)

```
Quick question for the shop owners in here --

When you're on a job and your phone rings with a new customer calling, what do you actually do?

A) Let it go to voicemail and call back later
B) Stop what I'm doing and answer it
C) My office staff handles it
D) I have an answering service
E) Something else (drop it in the comments)

Genuinely curious. I've been thinking a lot about how many calls we all miss during peak season and what it actually costs us. No wrong answers.
```

**Why it works:** Polls get massive engagement. Facebook's algorithm loves them. Every answer is a potential lead -- especially the A's and B's. You can DM anyone who answers A with: "Same here man, that was killing me. Finally figured out a solution. Want me to share what I did?"

---

### Post 2: The Honest Story (Vulnerability = Trust)

```
Alright I gotta be honest about something.

Last summer I tracked every missed call at my shop for 30 days. I thought we were answering most of them. We weren't even close.

Out of roughly 190 calls in a month, we missed 67 of them. 67. And when I followed up on the ones that left voicemails... most of them had already called someone else.

I did the rough math. Average ticket for us is around $350. Even if only half of those missed calls would've converted, that's over $11,000 in one month just... gone. Walked right out the door because nobody picked up the phone.

That was a gut punch. Been on a mission to fix it ever since.

Anyone else ever actually tracked their missed calls? The number might surprise you.
```

**Why it works:** Raw honesty. Real numbers. Makes every owner in the group think "wait... how many am I missing?" Does not mention any product. If people ask what he did to fix it, Wallace answers in DMs or comments: "I'll shoot you a message, it's kind of a longer story."

---

### Post 3: The Helpful Tip (Pure Value, Authority Builder)

```
Tip for anyone still sending every call to voicemail after hours:

Your customers are calling at night because that's when they're HOME and thinking about their HVAC. It doesn't mean it's an emergency. Most of the time they just want to get on the schedule for this week.

But here's the thing -- if they hit voicemail, 80% of them are calling the next company on Google before you wake up tomorrow.

A few things that helped us:
- We stopped using a generic voicemail greeting and recorded one that says exactly when we'll call back (within 2 hours of the next business day)
- We set up a system so after-hours calls still get answered live and the caller can book right then
- We started texting missed callers back within 5 minutes even on nights/weekends (even an auto-text helps)

The phone is the front door of your business. If nobody answers the door, people leave.

What's everyone else doing for after-hours calls? Curious how other shops handle it.
```

**Why it works:** Gives 3 real tips (only one of which is The Call Taker, and it's not named). Positions Wallace as someone who has solved this problem. The last line invites discussion. When someone says "what system are you using for after-hours?" -- that's the opening.

---

### Post 4: The Controversial Take (Drives Comments and Debate)

```
Hot take: hiring a full-time receptionist is one of the worst investments a small HVAC shop can make.

Before you come at me -- hear me out.

A receptionist costs $30-40k/year. They work 8-5. They call in sick. They take lunch breaks. They quit and you start over.

Meanwhile, 40% of your calls come in outside business hours. Weekends. Holidays. 9pm when a condenser dies.

So you're paying $35k/year for someone who's not even there when half your calls come in.

I'm not saying customer service doesn't matter. I'm saying the opposite -- it matters so much that you can't rely on one human sitting at one desk during one shift to handle it.

Disagree? Tell me why. I want to hear the other side.
```

**Why it works:** Controversial posts get 5-10x more comments than normal posts. Owners with receptionists will defend their choice. Owners without them will agree. Both sides engage. Wallace can respond to every comment thoughtfully, building relationships. Nobody is being sold anything -- but the seed is planted that there's a better way.

### Post 5: The Weekend/Holiday Check-In (Timely, Seasonal)

```
It's [Saturday/holiday weekend] and my phone has already rung 6 times this morning.

2 people wanting quotes on new systems
1 emergency no-heat call
1 person asking about financing
2 wanting to get on the schedule for next week

If I was still handling phones the old way, I would've missed at least 4 of those while I was on the first call or out on the emergency.

Just a reminder -- your busiest call times are often when you're least available to answer. Weekends and evenings are gold mines if you have a way to capture those calls.

How's everyone else's phone ringing this weekend? Busy or slow?
```

**Why it works:** Timely and relatable. Posts about the current day/weekend feel authentic. Shows that Wallace runs a real HVAC shop and deals with the same things everyone else does. The phrase "handling phones the old way" creates curiosity without naming the new way. Any "what are you using?" question is an invitation to DM.
