## Section 1: How to Find HVAC Owners on LinkedIn

### Exact Search Terms

Use LinkedIn's search bar with these queries (one at a time):

1. `HVAC owner Nashville`
2. `HVAC company president Tennessee`
3. `heating and air owner Nashville`
4. `HVAC business founder Tennessee`
5. `air conditioning company owner Nashville`
6. `HVAC contractor Nashville TN`
7. `heating and cooling owner Tennessee`
8. `mechanical contractor owner Nashville`

### Filters to Apply

After searching, click "People" then "All Filters" and set:

- **Locations:** Nashville, Tennessee Metropolitan Area (this captures Lebanon, Murfreesboro, Franklin, Gallatin, Hendersonville, Mt. Juliet, Clarksville, and surrounding cities)
- **Industry:** Construction, Building Materials, Facilities Services
- **Title keywords:** Owner, President, Founder, CEO, General Manager, Partner
- **Connections:** 2nd and 3rd+ (skip 1st — you already know them)
- **Company size:** 1-10, 11-50, 51-200 employees (small to mid-size shops)

### How to Expand Beyond Nashville

Once you've worked through Nashville, repeat the same searches for:

- Memphis, TN
- Knoxville, TN
- Chattanooga, TN
- Huntsville, AL
- Bowling Green, KY
- Atlanta, GA (huge market)

### Daily Connection Request Limits

LinkedIn will restrict or ban your account if you go too hard. Stay safe:

- **Free LinkedIn account:** 10-15 connection requests per day max
- **LinkedIn Sales Navigator ($99/mo):** 20-25 per day max (better filters, worth it when you're ready to scale)
- **Never exceed 100 per week** regardless of account type
- **Spread them out** — don't send 15 all at once. Do 5 in the morning, 5 in the evening
- **Skip weekends** — acceptance rates drop, and it looks automated

### Best Times to Send

| Time Window | Why |
|---|---|
| Tuesday - Thursday, 7:00-8:00 AM CST | HVAC owners check their phones before heading to the first job |
| Tuesday - Thursday, 12:00-1:00 PM CST | Lunch break — scrolling LinkedIn in the truck |
| Tuesday - Thursday, 7:00-9:00 PM CST | Done for the day, on the couch, catching up on messages |

**Worst times:** Monday mornings (inbox overload), Friday afternoons (checked out), weekends (low activity).

## Section 2: Connection Request Notes (5 Variations)

LinkedIn limits connection request notes to 300 characters. These are short, warm, and zero sales pressure. Your only goal is to get them to hit "Accept."

---

**Variation 1** (194 characters)
> Hey [First Name], fellow business owner in the Nashville area. I work with HVAC companies on the operations side. Always looking to connect with people in the trades. Hope you're having a good week.

---

**Variation 2** (176 characters)
> Hey [First Name] — saw you run [Company Name]. I work with HVAC companies here in Middle Tennessee. No pitch, just like connecting with other local business owners. Cheers.

---

**Variation 3** (173 characters)
> [First Name], I'm out of Lebanon, TN and work with a handful of HVAC companies in the area. Always good to know other business owners in the trades. Would love to connect.

---

**Variation 4** (189 characters)
> Hey [First Name] — noticed we're both in the Nashville area. I help HVAC companies with their phone systems and figured we should be connected. Either way, hope business is going well.

---

**Variation 5** (147 characters)
> [First Name] — fellow Nashville-area business owner here. I work in the HVAC space and like connecting with others in the industry. Cheers.

---

**Tips for Connection Requests:**
- Use their first name, always
- Mention their company name or city when you can (shows you're not mass-blasting)
- Never mention pricing, AI, or "demo" in the connection request
- If their profile has something specific (a post, an award, a milestone), mention it instead — that beats any template

## Section 3: First Message After They Accept (5 Variations)

Send this **24-48 hours** after they accept your connection. Not immediately (looks desperate). Not a week later (they forgot who you are). The sweet spot is the next day.

**Your only goal here: start a conversation by asking a question they can easily answer.**

---

**Variation 1: The Direct Question**

> Hey [First Name], thanks for connecting. Quick question for you — how are you guys handling after-hours calls over at [Company Name]? Voicemail? Answering service? Just curious, it comes up a lot with the HVAC companies I work with.

---

**Variation 2: The Observation**

> [First Name] — appreciate the connection. I see you guys are doing your thing over at [Company Name]. Respect.
>
> Random question: when you're on a job and the phone rings, what happens to that call? That's been a huge topic with the HVAC owners I've been talking to lately.

---

**Variation 3: The Seasonal Angle**

> Hey [First Name], glad we connected. Quick one for you — with [summer/busy season] coming up, do you have a plan for when the phones start ringing off the hook and your guys are all out on jobs? Curious how you handle it at [Company Name].

---

**Variation 4: The Stat Drop**

> Hey [First Name], thanks for connecting. I was just reading a study that said 85% of callers who hit voicemail don't leave a message — they just call the next company. Crazy stat. How do you guys handle calls at [Company Name] when everyone's out in the field?

---

**Variation 5: The Casual Check-In**

> [First Name] — appreciate the connect. How's business going over at [Company Name]? Things picking up heading into [season]?
>
> I work with HVAC companies on the phone side of things. Always curious how shops your size are handling the call volume.

---

**Tips for First Messages:**
- Always end with a question — it invites a reply
- Keep it to 2-4 sentences max. This is LinkedIn, not email
- Never link your website, send a phone number, or mention pricing in this message
- If they viewed your profile before accepting, they're curious — this message is your chance

## Section 5: Follow-Up If They DON'T Respond (2 Messages)

Some people get busy. Some people saw it and forgot. Some people aren't interested. Two follow-ups max, then you move on.

---

### Day 5 Bump (Different Angle, Keep It Short)

> Hey [First Name] — didn't mean to leave you hanging. Totally different topic: have you ever called your own business after 6pm just to see what happens?
>
> Most HVAC owners I've talked to have never done it. Try it tonight. Call your main line at 8pm. If a customer gets the same experience you get, you're probably losing 2-3 jobs a week without knowing it.

> Either way, no worries — just thought it was worth mentioning.

---

### Day 14 Final Message (Leave the Demo Line, Bow Out)

> [First Name] — I'll keep this short and get out of your hair. If you ever want to hear what an AI receptionist sounds like for an HVAC company, call this number anytime: (615) 784-5747.
>
> It answers 24/7, books appointments, and texts you the details. $297/mo flat rate. No pressure, no follow-up from me. The number's there whenever you're ready.
>
> Hope [Company Name] has a killer [season]. Take care.

---

**Rules for Follow-Ups:**
- Never send more than 2 follow-ups total
- Never guilt-trip ("I've messaged you twice now...")
- Never send the same message twice — change the angle each time
- If they still don't respond after the Day 14 message, they're a "not now." Add them to a list and re-engage in 3-6 months with a completely fresh approach
- If they viewed your message but didn't reply, that's not a no — it's a "not right now." The Day 14 message with the demo number plants the seed

## Profile Optimization (Do This First)

Before you send a single connection request, make sure your LinkedIn profile is dialed in:

- **Headline:** Don't say "CEO of The Call Taker." Say: `I help HVAC companies stop losing jobs to voicemail | AI Receptionist | Nashville, TN`
- **Profile photo:** Professional headshot, friendly, approachable. Not a logo.
- **Banner image:** The Call Taker branding with the tagline and demo number
- **About section:** 3-4 sentences. Who you help, what the problem is, how you solve it. End with the demo number.
- **Featured section:** Pin your best LinkedIn post (use Post 2 or Post 3 from Section 6) and a link to thecalltaker.com
- **Experience:** List The Call Taker with a description that reads like a pitch, not a resume

Your profile IS your landing page on LinkedIn. When an HVAC owner gets your connection request, the first thing they do is click your name. Make sure what they see builds trust, not confusion.
