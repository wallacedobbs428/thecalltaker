## SECTION 5: POST-PODCAST FOLLOW-UP STRATEGY

### The Day the Episode Drops

**Within 2 hours of the episode going live:**

1. **Share on every platform.** Post about the episode on Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, and X (Twitter). Each post should be slightly different --- not a copy-paste across platforms.

2. **Tag the host and the show** in every post. Use their handles, not just their names. This increases the chance they reshare your post to their audience.

3. **Pin the post** on your Facebook page and LinkedIn profile for at least one week.

4. **Send a personal thank-you message** to the host. Not a public post --- a private DM or email. Something like: *"Hey [name], the episode just dropped and it looks great. Really appreciate you having me on. If there's anything I can do to help promote it, just say the word."*

5. **Email your existing customer list and leads.** Subject line: *"I was just on [SHOW NAME] --- here's what we talked about."* Link to the episode. This gives your customers a reason to engage and shows prospects that you're a recognized voice in the industry.

### Week 1: Repurpose the Content

6. **Pull 3--5 short clips** (60--90 seconds each) from the interview. These become standalone social media content. Focus on moments where Wallace says something surprising, funny, or highly tactical. Use a tool like Opus Clip, Descript, or hire a freelancer on Fiverr.

7. **Write a blog post or LinkedIn article** that expands on one key talking point from the interview. Title it something like: *"What I Told [HOST NAME] About Missed Calls in HVAC."* Link back to the full episode.

8. **Create a quote graphic** --- take the single best one-liner from the episode, put it on a branded graphic, and post it. Tag the host.

9. **Add the episode to The Call Taker website.** Create a "Press" or "As Seen On" section. Even one podcast logo adds credibility. As the list grows, this becomes a powerful trust signal.

### Week 2--4: Extended Promotion

10. **Share one clip per week** for the next 3--4 weeks. Don't dump all the clips at once. Space them out so you get sustained visibility from a single interview.

11. **Use clips in paid ads** (with the host's permission). A 60-second clip of Wallace on a respected podcast, run as a Facebook ad to HVAC owners, is more credible than any ad you could script yourself.

12. **Reference the episode in sales conversations.** When talking to prospects: *"I was just on [SHOW NAME] talking about this exact problem --- I can send you the episode if you want to hear the full breakdown."* This positions Wallace as a thought leader, not just a salesperson.

### Ongoing: Build the Relationship

13. **Engage with the host's content regularly.** Comment on their posts, share their other episodes, support their work. This is a long-term relationship, not a one-time transaction.

14. **Offer to come back.** After 3--6 months, reach out with a new angle or fresh data. Say: *"Hey [name], since I was on the show, we've seen [new data/insight]. Would you be interested in a follow-up episode?"* Most hosts love having returning guests because it's easy to book and the audience already knows them.

15. **Ask for referrals to other shows.** After building a genuine relationship with the host, ask: *"Are there any other podcasts in the space that you think would be a good fit for me? Happy to mention [SHOW NAME] as how I got connected."* Podcast hosts know each other. One warm intro is worth more than 50 cold pitches.

### The Podcast Guest Flywheel

The goal is not "get on one podcast." The goal is to build a flywheel:

```
Get on Podcast A
    --> Repurpose content for 4 weeks of social media
    --> Use the appearance as credibility to pitch Podcasts B, C, D
    --> Build relationship with Host A
    --> Host A refers you to Host B
    --> Repeat
```

After 5--10 podcast appearances, Wallace becomes the "AI phone guy" that every HVAC podcast host thinks of when the topic comes up. That is the real asset --- not any single episode, but the cumulative reputation.
