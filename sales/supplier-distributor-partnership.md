## 1. WHY SUPPLY HOUSES SHOULD CARE

### The Core Argument: When Your Contractors Win, You Win

Every HVAC supply house, distributor, and manufacturer rep depends on one thing: contractors buying equipment and parts. The more jobs a contractor books, the more equipment and materials they purchase. It is that simple.

The biggest threat to your contractors' revenue is not competition, pricing, or even the economy. It is missed phone calls.

**The numbers:**

- The average HVAC company misses 20-40% of inbound calls (industry data from ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro)
- 80% of callers who reach voicemail hang up without leaving a message
- 78% of customers hire the first company that responds (Lead Connect)
- The average missed HVAC call represents $300-$800 in lost service revenue
- A single contractor missing 25 calls/month loses $9,000-$15,000/month in potential revenue

**What that means for you as a distributor:**

Every missed call is a job that does not get booked. Every job that does not get booked is equipment that does not get sold, parts that do not get ordered, and refrigerant that stays on your shelf.

If one of your contractor customers is missing 30 calls a month, that is potentially 18 jobs they are not running. If even 5 of those jobs would have resulted in an equipment sale, system replacement, or significant parts order, you are leaving substantial revenue on the table — not the contractor. You.

**The Call Taker solves this.** It is an AI-powered receptionist built specifically for HVAC companies. It answers every call, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. It books appointments, captures customer information, handles emergency dispatch, and integrates with scheduling software. Plans start at $297/month.

**When you recommend The Call Taker to your contractor base, here is what happens:**

1. Your contractors book more jobs
2. More jobs mean more equipment and parts purchases from you
3. You earn a referral commission on every contractor who signs up
4. You differentiate yourself from competing distributors by offering business growth tools, not just products
5. Your contractors become more successful, more loyal, and less likely to switch suppliers

This is not charity. This is a business development strategy that puts more money in your register.

## 4. COMMISSION & REFERRAL STRUCTURE

### Standard Referral Program

| Tier | Active Referrals | Monthly Commission Per Subscriber | Annual Value Per Subscriber |
|------|-----------------|----------------------------------|---------------------------|
| Bronze | 1-10 contractors | $50/month | $600/year |
| Silver | 11-25 contractors | $60/month | $720/year |
| Gold | 26-50 contractors | $75/month | $900/year |
| Platinum | 51+ contractors | $85/month + quarterly bonus | $1,020/year + bonus |

### How It Works

1. **Unique Referral Link/Code:** Each distributor partner receives a unique tracking link and referral code. When a contractor signs up using that link or code, the referral is attributed to the distributor.

2. **Commission Payment:** Commissions are paid monthly via ACH or check, 30 days in arrears. Distributor receives a monthly statement showing all active subscribers, plan levels, and commission earned.

3. **Residual Model:** Commissions are residual — the distributor earns every month the contractor remains a subscriber. Average subscriber retention in SaaS for service businesses is 14-18 months, making the lifetime value of each referral significant.

4. **No Caps:** There is no limit on the number of contractors a distributor can refer. A distributor with 500 contractor customers who converts 50 to The Call Taker at the Gold tier earns $3,750/month ($45,000/year) in passive referral income.

## 5. TOP 10 HVAC SUPPLY CHAINS — APPROACH STRATEGY

### 1. Johnstone Supply
**Overview:** Largest HVAC/R distribution cooperative in the US. 450+ locations. Independently owned member stores.
**Approach:** Because stores are independently owned, approach at the local/regional level first. Identify the most active stores in your target markets. Pitch to the store owner/manager directly. Success at individual locations creates case studies for approaching the cooperative at a national level. Johnstone hosts an annual member meeting — target that event for broader exposure.
**Key Contact Titles:** Store Owner, Store Manager, Marketing Coordinator
**Entry Point:** Walk into the local branch. Ask to speak with the owner or manager. Bring a counter card and flyer sample. Offer a lunch & learn.

### 2. Ferguson?"hvac (Ferguson Enterprises)
**Overview:** Massive distributor across plumbing, HVAC, and industrial. Part of Ferguson Enterprises (publicly traded). Multiple HVAC-focused branches.
**Approach:** More corporate structure than Johnstone. Start with the branch manager at local HVAC-focused locations. Build a relationship and results at branch level before approaching regional or corporate management. Ferguson has dedicated HVAC sales reps who visit contractor shops — getting a recommendation from those reps is the highest-value play.
**Key Contact Titles:** Branch Manager, HVAC Sales Representative, Regional Manager
**Entry Point:** Schedule a meeting with the branch manager. Position as a vendor partnership that helps their customers grow. Ask to present at their next contractor event.

### 3. Winsupply
**Overview:** 700+ locations nationally. Like Johnstone, operates as a system of locally owned companies. Covers HVAC, plumbing, industrial, and waterworks.
**Approach:** Similar to Johnstone — locally owned means local decision-making. Approach individual Winsupply HVAC locations. The local ownership model means the person behind the counter may also be the person who approves partnerships. Quick, efficient sales cycle at the local level.
**Key Contact Titles:** Local Company President (owner), Branch Manager, Counter Manager
**Entry Point:** In-person visit to the local Winsupply HVAC location. Direct pitch to the owner.

### 4. ACR Group (ACR Supply, T&S, Team Air Distributing)
**Overview:** Regional distributor, strong in the South and Southeast. Multiple brands under the ACR Group umbrella.
**Approach:** Regional player, so decisions can be made faster than national chains. Target branch managers and regional sales managers. Their contractor customers are typically small to mid-size — exactly the demographic that benefits most from The Call Taker. Position as a value-add that helps ACR differentiate from bigger distributors.
**Key Contact Titles:** Branch Manager, Regional Sales Manager, VP of Sales
**Entry Point:** Email to regional sales leadership followed by an in-branch demo.

### 5. Gemaire Distributors (Watsco subsidiary)
**Overview:** Major distributor of Carrier, Bryant, and other brands. Primarily in the Southeast and expanding. Part of Watsco, the largest HVAC distributor in the US.
**Approach:** Watsco is publicly traded and has a dealer development focus. They actively invest in tools and programs that help their dealers succeed. Position The Call Taker as a dealer success tool. Approach at the branch level first, but the Watsco corporate structure may also have a dealer programs or business development team that evaluates vendor partnerships.
**Key Contact Titles:** Branch Manager, Dealer Development Manager, Territory Manager
**Entry Point:** Contact the local Gemaire branch. Ask about their dealer programs and how vendors can participate.

### 6. Lennox Industries (Lennox Stores / PartsPlus)
**Overview:** Manufacturer with company-owned distribution (Lennox Stores) and independent dealer network. PartsPlus stores serve non-Lennox dealers.
**Approach:** Two tracks: (1) Approach Lennox Stores directly for counter placement and contractor recommendations. (2) Approach Lennox corporate about inclusion in their dealer programs — Lennox invests heavily in dealer success tools and could integrate The Call Taker as a recommended vendor. Lennox hosts dealer meetings and conferences — these are prime events.
**Key Contact Titles:** Store Manager (Lennox Stores), Dealer Programs Manager, Territory Manager
**Entry Point:** Local Lennox Store manager for branch-level placement. Lennox corporate dealer programs team for network-wide partnership.

### 7. Baker Distributing (Watsco subsidiary)
**Overview:** Another Watsco company. Distributes multiple brands. Strong in the Southeast and Midwest.
**Approach:** Same Watsco corporate structure as Gemaire. Approach locally through branch managers. Baker locations tend to have strong relationships with their contractor customers, making counter recommendations highly effective. Ask to participate in their open houses and contractor appreciation events.
**Key Contact Titles:** Branch Manager, Territory Sales Manager
**Entry Point:** In-person branch visit, request to place materials and present at next contractor event.

### 8. Carrier Enterprise (Watsco subsidiary)
**Overview:** Largest Carrier distributor in the US. Watsco joint venture. Hundreds of locations.

### 9. RE Michel Company
**Overview:** Employee-owned distributor. 300+ locations in the Eastern US. HVAC/R parts and equipment.
**Approach:** Employee-owned culture means decisions may be more relationship-driven and community-oriented. Approach through local branches with a personal touch. The employee-ownership angle means staff are invested in the business — they may be more receptive to tools that help their customers succeed. Emphasize the referral commission as a benefit to the branch.
**Key Contact Titles:** Branch Manager, Regional Manager, Sales Representative
**Entry Point:** In-person visit, personal relationship building, referral to regional management.

### 10. US Air Conditioning Distributors (?"usacd)
**Overview:** West Coast focused distributor. Strong in California, Arizona, Nevada, and surrounding states.
**Approach:** Regional focus makes them easier to approach than national chains. West Coast HVAC market has high average tickets and long cooling seasons, making the ROI argument for The Call Taker even stronger. Contractor customers in California often face high competition and need every advantage.
**Key Contact Titles:** Branch Manager, Sales Manager, Marketing Director
**Entry Point:** Contact their main office or visit a local branch. Position The Call Taker as a competitive advantage tool for their contractors in a crowded West Coast market.

### General Approach Principles for All Distributors

1. **Always start local.** Walk into the branch. Relationships start in person.
2. **Bring samples.** Have a counter card and flyer ready. Make it tangible.
3. **Lead with their benefit.** Do not lead with what The Call Taker does. Lead with what it does for the distributor: more equipment sales, happier contractors, referral revenue.
4. **Ask about events.** Every distributor does open houses, appreciation days, or dealer meetings. Get on the calendar.
5. **Follow up consistently.** Distributors are busy. It may take 2-3 touches to get a meeting. Be persistent without being pushy.
6. **Get a champion.** Find one person at the branch who believes in it. Counter staff who see contractors every day are ideal champions — especially if they earn a spiff.

### Supply House Open House Strategy (Lowest Cost, Highest Frequency)

Supply house open houses happen regularly — most do at least 2-4 per year. These are the most accessible and cost-effective events to attend.

**How to get invited:**
1. Build a relationship with the branch manager (see Section 5)
2. Offer to bring food/refreshments for the event (cost: $100-200)
3. Ask for a small table or counter space — you do not need a full booth
4. Volunteer to do a 10-15 minute "business tip" presentation during the event

**At the event:**
- Small table with counter cards, flyers, and a phone for live demos
- Talk to every contractor. Ask about their call volume and after-hours coverage.
- Focus on getting them to call the demo line right there. The live experience converts.
- Collect business cards or scan info for follow-up.

**Expected results:** A typical supply house open house has 30-80 contractor attendees. Converting 5-10% to The Call Taker subscribers is a realistic target from a single event.
