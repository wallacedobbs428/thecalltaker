OK -- I see some numbers coming in. Let's do the math together, and I'm going to use conservative numbers on purpose.

**Here's the formula.**

Let's say your company gets **40 calls per week**. That's pretty average for a company running two to five trucks.

Now, let's say you're missing **25 percent** of those calls. That's ten calls a week that go to voicemail, get a busy signal, or ring out because your team is on the other line, out on a job, or it's after hours.

Out of those ten missed calls, how many do you think call back? Industry research says **less than half**. Most of them call the next company on Google. So let's say you permanently lose **six of those ten calls every single week**.

What's the average revenue from an HVAC service call? Depending on your market, it's somewhere between **250 and 500 dollars** for a standard service call. If it turns into a repair, you're looking at 800, maybe 1,200 dollars. If it turns into a system replacement, you're talking 6,000 to 15,000 dollars.

Let's be **extremely conservative** and say the average value of a booked call is **$350**.

So: 6 lost calls per week times $350 equals **$2,100 per week** in lost revenue.

Multiply that by 52 weeks and you get **$109,200 per year**.

*Pause. Let that number sit for a moment.*

Now, I used conservative numbers on purpose. If your average ticket is higher, or you're missing more calls, or you're a bigger shop -- this number gets a lot bigger, fast.

Let me show you what this looks like for different size companies.

**A one-to-two truck operation** getting maybe 20 calls a week, missing 25 percent: you're looking at roughly **$54,600 per year** in lost revenue.

**A three-to-five truck operation** getting 40 calls a week: roughly **$109,200 per year**.

**A six-plus truck operation** getting 75 or more calls a week: you could be leaving **$200,000 or more on the table** every single year.

And here's the part that really stings. You already **paid** to get those calls. You're running Google Ads. You've got your Google Business Profile. You're paying for SEO. You're wrapped trucks. You're doing mailers. All of that money you spent on marketing -- and then when the phone rings, nobody's there to answer it.

It's like filling a bucket with a hole in the bottom. You can pour more water in, but you're still losing it.

Now -- what if you could patch that hole? What if every single call that came into your business got answered, professionally, on the first ring, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, 365 days a year? # HOW TO HOST IT

## Recommended Platform: Zoom (Free Plan)

**Why Zoom is the best free option:**

1. **Familiarity.** Most people have used Zoom. No learning curve for attendees.
2. **Free plan supports up to 100 attendees** for up to 40 minutes (your webinar is 30 minutes -- fits perfectly).
3. **Built-in registration page.** Zoom lets you create a registration form that collects names and emails automatically.
4. **Chat feature.** Attendees can drop questions in the chat during Q&A.
5. **Recording.** You can record the webinar and use it as a replay for follow-up emails.
6. **Screen sharing.** You'll need this for the slide deck and potentially for the live demo.
7. **Waiting room.** Keeps things professional and lets you control when attendees enter.

**Alternatives:**

- **YouTube Live** -- Good if you expect more than 100 attendees (no limit). But it's less interactive, and the chat can be chaotic. Also requires a YouTube channel with live streaming enabled (may take 24 hours to activate if you haven't done it before). Use this if your audience skews larger.
- **Google Meet** -- Free and simple, but no built-in registration, no recording on the free plan, and a 100-participant limit. Not recommended for this type of event.
- **StreamYard + YouTube Live** -- Best combo if you want a polished, professional-looking stream with overlays, banners, and a lower-third with your name/website. Free plan allows one destination (YouTube). Good option if you want to look more produced.
