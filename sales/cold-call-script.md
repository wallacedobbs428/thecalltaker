# The Call Taker — Cold Call Script

## Before You Dial

- Know the company name and the owner's first name (check their website, Google listing, or Yelp).
- Best calling times: 7:00-8:00 AM (before they head out) or 5:00-6:30 PM (wrapping up the day).
- Have your demo booking link ready.
- Stand up or walk around. Sound like a real person, not a telemarketer.

---

## The Script

### Opening (15 seconds)

> "Hey [Name], this is Wallace with The Call Taker. Quick question — when someone calls your shop after hours, what happens?"

**Then SHUT UP and listen.** Let them answer. Their answer tells you everything.

**If they say "voicemail":**
> "Yeah, that's what I figured. That's actually why I'm calling."

**If they say "it goes to my cell":**
> "So you're answering calls at 9pm while you're trying to have dinner? I get it — I came from the trades. That's exhausting."

**If they say "we have an answering service":**
> "Got it. Are you happy with it? Because most guys I talk to say their answering service misses half the details and costs a fortune."

---

### The Problem (30 seconds)

> "Here's what I'm seeing with HVAC companies like yours. After hours, weekends, when you're up on a roof — calls are coming in and nobody's picking up. Or they're going to voicemail. And 85% of people won't leave a voicemail. They just hang up and call the next company in their Google results."
>
> "At $350 a job, even a few missed calls a week adds up fast. We're talking thousands of dollars a month that just disappears."

---

### The Solution (30 seconds)

> "So I built something called The Call Taker. It's an AI receptionist that answers every call to your business — 24/7, 365 days a year. It picks up in 2 seconds, talks to the customer, gets their name, address, and what's going on. It books the appointment right on your calendar. And then it texts you all the details immediately."
>
> "No voicemail. No missed calls. No more losing jobs because nobody picked up the phone."

---

### Social Proof (15 seconds)

> "We've got HVAC companies using this right now that picked up an extra $10,000-$15,000 a month in jobs they would have lost. These are calls that were going to voicemail before. Now every single one gets answered and booked."

---

### The Ask (15 seconds)

> "I'd love to show you a quick demo. Takes about 15 minutes. You'll actually call the AI yourself, talk to it like you're a customer with a broken AC, and see what happens on the back end. If you like it, great. If not, no hard feelings."
>
> "Do you have 15 minutes this week? I've got [Day] at [Time] or [Day] at [Time] — which works better?"

---

## Objection Handling

### 1. "I already have an answering service."

> "Totally fair. Can I ask — what do you pay for that? Most answering services run $200-$500 a month and the people answering don't know HVAC from plumbing. They take a message, maybe get the name right, and that's it."
>
> "The Call Taker costs less, never calls in sick, never puts anyone on hold, and actually books the appointment directly on your calendar. Your customer gets taken care of on the spot instead of waiting for a callback."
>
> "Worth a 15-minute look to compare?"

### 2. "I can't afford it."

> "I hear you. Let me ask you this — how many calls would you say go unanswered in a month? Even a rough guess."
>
> [Let them answer]
>
> "OK, so if even 5 of those were real jobs at $350 each, that's $1,750 in revenue you're not getting. The Call Taker costs a fraction of that. It literally pays for itself with the first couple of recovered calls."
>
> "It's not an expense. It's a revenue tool. Can I show you the math on a quick demo?"

### 3. "AI sounds robotic / My customers won't like it."

> "That's the number one thing people say before they hear it. And then they call it and go, 'Wait, that's the AI?' It doesn't sound like a robot. It sounds like someone at your front desk."
>
> "That's exactly why I do live demos. You call it yourself, talk to it like a customer, and judge for yourself. If it sounds robotic to you, I'll leave you alone. Fair enough?"

### 4. "I don't need it. I answer my own calls."

> "Respect that. You're on top of it. But let me ask — what happens when you're on a roof? Or you're in a crawl space? Or it's 10pm and your phone rings? Even if you catch 90% of calls, that other 10% is real money."
>
> "And honestly — should you be answering phones while you're trying to run a business? Every call you take is time off the job. The Call Taker handles that so you can focus on the work."
>
> "Worth a quick look?"

### 5. "Let me think about it."

> "Totally fair. I never want to pressure anyone. But let me ask you this — while you're thinking about it, how many calls are going to hit voicemail tonight? Tomorrow? This weekend?"
>
> "How about this: let me set up a quick 15-minute demo so you can actually see it and hear it. That way you're making a decision based on what it actually does, not just what I'm telling you on the phone. No commitment — just a look."
>
> "I've got [Day] at [Time]. Does that work?"

### 6. "How much does it cost?"

> "It depends on your setup — how many trucks, call volume, that kind of thing. But I can tell you it's a fraction of what an answering service costs and it does ten times more."
>
> "The best way to talk pricing is on the demo, because I can show you exactly what you're getting and give you a number based on your specific business. Fair?"

---

## Closing

**If they agree to a demo:**

> "Awesome. I'm going to send you a calendar invite right now. You'll also get a confirmation text. When we get on the call, I'll have you actually dial the AI and talk to it — it's the best part. Looking forward to it, [Name]."

**If they say no / not now:**

> "No problem at all, [Name]. I appreciate you taking the call. Mind if I follow up in about a week? I'll shoot you a quick text or email — nothing pushy. I just think once you see it, it's going to click."
>
> [If they agree to follow-up, log the date and set a reminder]

**If it's a hard no:**

> "Understood. If anything changes or you start noticing those missed calls adding up, you've got my number. Take care, [Name]."

- [ ] Log the call: company name, owner name, result, objections raised
- [ ] If demo booked: send calendar invite within 5 minutes
- [ ] If follow-up agreed: set reminder for the date
- [ ] If voicemail: leave a 20-second message and add to email sequence
- [ ] Update the secret shopper log if applicable
